# AI Intelligence Briefing — 2026-08-03

## Top News

**OpenAI announced on August 1 that an internal version of its next major model, Astra, solved ten longstanding open problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science, each unsolved for at least a decade, and published a 249-page manuscript with machine-checkable Lean 4 certificates on GitHub under Apache 2.0.** The headline result is the first-ever explicit construction of a non-sofic group — a question open since Mikhail Gromov introduced soficity in 1999. Additional results include a disproof of Connes's rigidity conjecture, a proof of Ehrhart's volume conjecture, resolutions of three Paul Erdős problems including multicolored Ramsey numbers, the first improvement to high-dimensional sphere-packing bounds since 1978, a parallel repetition theorem for quantum games, and new circuit complexity lower bounds. The total compute cost was approximately $2,000 at current API rates. Astra is a multi-agent system — a root agent spawns and coordinates subagents over hours or days — and is not yet publicly available. Fields Medal winner Timothy Gowers said he would recommend one proof for publication in a top journal without hesitation; Thomas Bloom, who maintains the erdosproblems.com database, called the results "big news." OpenAI also demonstrated Astra to Washington policymakers this week. Sources: The Next Web, SiliconANGLE, QZ, TechTimes (August 1–3).

**The EU AI Act's GPAI enforcement powers went live on August 2, activating the European Commission's authority to fine providers of general-purpose AI models and marking the end of a year-long grace period since the GPAI rules first applied in August 2025.** The Commission can now demand model documentation, run technical evaluations, require compliance measures, restrict or withdraw a model from the EU market, and issue fines up to 3% of global annual turnover or €15 million — whichever is higher. Transparency requirements include disclosing how models were built, what copyrighted content was used in training, and what capability limitations downstream users should know. Anthropic and OpenAI were named today among the companies facing new scrutiny; Meta notably did not sign the voluntary GPAI Code of Practice. A significant enforcement gap remains: only 8 of the EU's 27 member states have designated the required national competent authorities, which means enforcement will be uneven at launch. The Brussels Effect — the EU's tendency to impose de facto global standards on companies with EU market exposure — gives this milestone reach well beyond Europe. Sources: Euronews, CNBC, AIFOD, beam.ai (August 2–3).

**Today's Signal:** Two structurally different AI milestones landed within 48 hours: Astra demonstrates that AI can now act as an autonomous research instrument capable of original contributions at the frontier of mathematics, not just assistance; the EU AI Act enforcement trigger establishes that regulators can now demand accountability for how those systems were built and what they were trained on. The gap between what AI claims to achieve and what any external party can verify — a gap the Leiden Declaration was written to address — is now a live regulatory question, not just a scientific one.

## Notable Model Releases

**DeepSeek released DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 on July 31 — the production version of its 284-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts agentic model, trained entirely through post-training improvements to the April preview's pipeline — and it outperforms DeepSeek's own V4-Pro-Preview on all nine agent and coding benchmarks the company published.** On Terminal-Bench 2.1, the 0731 build scores 82.7 versus 61.8 for the April preview; on CyberGym it scores 76.7, surpassing the larger V4-Pro-Preview despite being the cheaper tier. The model runs 13 billion active parameters per token, supports a 1-million-token context window, and is priced at $0.14/$0.28 per million input/output tokens (with cache hit input at $0.003). At max reasoning effort, its cost per task is approximately $0.03 — comparable to OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Luna at similar intelligence scores but at 2–3× lower cost. Codex Responses API and standard chat API are both supported. Two Hacker News threads reached 507 and 353 points on July 31. Sources: MarkTechPost, TechTimes, Artificial Analysis, aireleasetracker.com (July 31).

**MiniMax released H3, its new video generation model, on July 31 — ranking #1 in video editing and top 3 in both text-to-video and image-to-video on Artificial Analysis, making it the leading open-weight video model by benchmark.** H3 is an omni-modal model that accepts text, images, video, and audio input and generates up to 15-second clips at 2K resolution and 24 fps with native stereo audio — positioning it against ByteDance's Seedance 2.5, which launched the same day as a closed model. The API costs $0.13 per second. Open weights are planned "within days, subject to applicable laws and regulations" under a MiniMax Community License; as of August 2, the weights had not shipped. Sources: Reuters, South China Morning Post, Artificial Analysis, Techmeme (July 31–August 1).

**Today's Signal:** DeepSeek V4-Flash 0731 is the clearest demonstration yet that post-training pipeline improvements can deliver benchmark leaps without architectural changes or new pretraining runs — a capability that inverts the conventional assumption that frontier gains require more compute. MiniMax H3's benchmark position, if the open-weight release holds, would make a #1-ranked video model freely self-hostable for the first time; the licensing trajectory (Modified MIT → Community License with increasing restrictions) is a risk factor builders should track before committing infrastructure.

## Funding & Product News

**UK photonic chip startup OLIX raised $312 million in a Series B at a $3.3 billion valuation on August 3 — more than tripling its $1 billion valuation from a February 2026 Series A — with backing from Arm, Fundomo, Hudson River Trading, Reed Hastings, and the UK government's Sovereign AI venture fund.** OLIX is building an Optical Tensor Processing Unit (OTPU) that processes AI workloads using photons rather than electrons, which it claims produces lower heat and higher throughput for inference workloads. The company is targeting first chip delivery to customers in H2 2027. The Sovereign AI fund's participation alongside Arm and Hudson River Trading reflects UK government interest in building a domestic alternative to TSMC-manufactured GPU supply chains. Sources: Data Center Dynamics, Electronics Weekly, Cryptobriefing, olix.com (August 3).

**Zenity, an Israeli startup providing security tooling for enterprise AI agents, raised $125 million in a Series C led by Norwest Venture Partners on August 2, bringing total funding to $185 million.** The company provides visibility, access control, and threat detection for AI agents deployed inside enterprise environments — a category that has grown from niche to urgent as agentic AI deployments have multiplied. Zenity reports tripling revenue annually. On the same day, San Francisco-based Horizon3 raised $250 million in a Series E at a $2 billion valuation from NightDragon and NEA; its NodeZero platform conducts continuous AI-driven penetration testing across enterprise infrastructure and is approaching $100 million in annual recurring revenue at 120% year-over-year growth. Sources: AI Weekly (August 2).

**Microsoft's Project Perception agentic security platform entered public preview today, August 3 — the date announced when the system was unveiled on July 27.** Project Perception coordinates three classes of specialized AI agents — red (attack-path hunting), blue (risk prioritization), and green (automated remediation) — running continuously alongside MAI-Cyber-1-Flash, Microsoft's purpose-built cybersecurity model that handles roughly 90% of routine security tasks before routing complex cases to GPT-5.4. Sources: Microsoft Blog, GeekWire, WindowsForum (July 27–August 3).

**Today's Signal:** The week's three largest AI funding rounds all land in infrastructure or security, not in applications: OLIX bets that photonic silicon can challenge TSMC-manufactured GPU economics for inference; Zenity and Horizon3 together signal that AI agent security has become a billable enterprise category, not just a research concern. The capital flow suggests that the near-term defensible value in AI is in the layer that protects and accelerates AI infrastructure rather than in the applications it runs.

## Discussions & Debates

**"OpenAI's amazing — but vastly oversold — new model Astra" (Hacker News item 49148959) drew hundreds of comments disputing the framing of the Astra math announcement, forming the most substantive technical critique of the week's biggest story.** The core argument in the skeptical thread: OpenAI described Astra solving problems "each unsolved for at least a decade" in language that implies frontier mathematical difficulty, but several of the ten problems are research-community known as tractable with sufficient compute, and none have cleared peer review. The Leiden Declaration — published June 2026, endorsed by the International Mathematical Union with over 3,000 signatories including Terence Tao and Peter Scholze — was directly invoked: it warns that AI companies are "using published research without consent, bypassing peer review, and threatening the integrity of proof and attribution." A parallel thread noted the structural issue: Lean 4 certificates make the proofs machine-verifiable for formal correctness, but formal correctness and mathematical significance are different claims, and the significance claim is made in a press release, not a journal. Thomas Bloom's endorsement ("big news") versus his prior criticism of OpenAI's October 2025 Erdős claims as "dramatic misrepresentation" was cited as evidence that even sympathetic expert evaluators apply substantially different standards depending on how results are framed. Sources: HN item 49148959, Leiden Declaration (leidendeclaration.ai), SiliconANGLE (August 2–3).

**Andrej Karpathy's post "More on the pelican on the bicycle" (X/Twitter; HN top story today) argues that the pelican test — Simon Willison's whimsical benchmark asking models to draw an SVG of a pelican on a bicycle — has nearly maxed out as a useful AI vibe check, and that the field needs new informal capability signals.** Karpathy's demonstration: he asked Claude Opus 5 to convert a single paragraph of Tolkien's opening into an interactive 3D browser scene; the model produced 5,500 lines of Three.js code in approximately two hours for about $10. His broader claim: AI-generated interactive worlds are now "nearly free" to produce on-demand, but the models still cannot directly verify their own video output and rely on screenshots — meaning the generation is cheap but validation remains a human responsibility. The Hacker News discussion extended the argument: participants noted that the pelican test, like the GPT-4 unicorn TikZ exercise in 2023, served as an informal spatial-reasoning vibe check during a period when model capabilities were genuinely uncertain; the claim that it has "maxed out" implies we have no agreed replacement for informal capability signaling at the next tier of task complexity. Sources: Karpathy/X, The Decoder, Benzinga (August 1–3).

**Today's Signal:** Both debates converge on the same structural gap: the tools for evaluating AI capability — informal vibe checks and peer-reviewed journals alike — are not keeping pace with the rate of AI claims. Karpathy identifies that informal benchmarks go stale fast; the Leiden Declaration identifies that formal benchmarks are being bypassed. The Astra announcement lands directly in that gap: machine-verifiable Lean proofs satisfy neither camp fully. The AI industry is producing results faster than the evaluation infrastructure needed to interpret them.
