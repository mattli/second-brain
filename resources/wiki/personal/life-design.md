# Life Design

> TLDR: Lasting life change requires identity-level transformation, not surface-level goal-setting. All behavior is goal-oriented (Adlerian teleology), and unconscious goals — often rooted in childhood conditioning and identity protection — keep people stuck. A cybernetics-informed model of intelligence (set goal → act → sense → compare → iterate) combined with structured self-interrogation can break the cycle and launch a new trajectory.

## Identity Before Action

The central claim is that outcomes follow identity, not the reverse. A bodybuilder doesn't grind to eat healthy — they can't see themselves living any other way. A CEO doesn't force themselves out of bed — lying around feels wrong. The insight: **if you want a specific outcome, you must adopt the lifestyle that produces it before you reach it.** People who say "I can't wait until I'm done losing weight so I can enjoy life again" are telling you they haven't changed — they're enduring a temporary deviation from their real identity, and they'll snap back.

True change makes old habits feel disgusting, because you develop a deep awareness of what those habits compound into over time.

## All Behavior Is Goal-Oriented

Drawing on Alfred Adler's teleological psychology: every action pursues a goal, whether conscious or not. When someone procrastinates, they aren't "lacking discipline" — they're pursuing a different goal, often self-protection from judgment that would come from finishing and sharing work. When someone stays in a dead-end job despite saying they want out, they're pursuing safety, predictability, and social cover.

The implication: **real change requires changing your goals**, not just your actions. And a goal isn't a line item on a to-do list — it's a lens of perception that determines what information, ideas, and resources you notice.

## The Identity Trap

Identity crystallizes through a predictable cycle:

1. You want to achieve a goal
2. You perceive reality through that goal's lens
3. You notice only "important" information that aids the goal
4. You act and receive reinforcing feedback
5. Behavior becomes automatic and unconscious
6. It becomes part of who you think you are ("I am the type of person who...")
7. You defend that identity to maintain psychological consistency
8. Identity shapes new goals, restarting the cycle

This process begins in childhood through survival-driven conformity to parental values. Parents teach through reward and punishment; you adopt their beliefs or get punished. Those parents carry their own conditioning from their parents, propagating patterns across generations.

Once physical survival needs are met, you shift to **conceptual survival** — protecting and reproducing your ideas and worldview. When identity feels threatened, you experience the same fight-or-flight response as physical threat. This is why political, religious, or lifestyle challenges feel like personal attacks.

## Ego Development Stages

Koe synthesizes Maslow's Hierarchy, Greuter's ego development stages, Spiral Dynamics, and Integral Theory into nine stages:

1. **Impulsive** — No separation between impulse and action
2. **Self-Protective** — Learning to look out for yourself in a dangerous world
3. **Conformist** — You are your group; its rules feel like reality
4. **Self-Aware** — Noticing your inner life doesn't match the exterior
5. **Conscientious** — Building your own system of principles
6. **Individualist** — Seeing that your principles were shaped by context
7. **Strategist** — Working with systems while aware of your own involvement
8. **Construct-Aware** — Seeing all frameworks, including identity, as useful fictions
9. **Unitive** — Separation between self and life dissolves

The transition between any two stages follows the same pattern, making the specific stage less important than understanding the transition mechanism itself.

## Cybernetics & Intelligence

Intelligence, defined as "the ability to get what you want out of life" (Naval Ravikant), maps onto cybernetics — from the Greek *kybernetikos*, "good at steering." An intelligent system:

- Has a goal
- Acts toward it
- Senses where it is
- Compares position to the goal
- Acts again based on feedback

Success requires three ingredients: **agency** (likelihood of acting), **opportunity** (a goal worth pursuing), and **intelligence** (the ability to iterate through the sense-compare-act loop). The mark of low intelligence is the inability to learn from mistakes — hitting a roadblock and quitting rather than experimenting with new approaches.

This connects to the wiki's broader treatment of feedback loops in [Loop Engineering](../tools/loop-engineering.md) — the same sense-compare-act structure underlies both personal goal pursuit and agentic system design.

## Three Phases of Identity Flip

Successful identity transformations follow a pattern:

1. **Dissonance** — Feeling like you don't belong in your current life; becoming sufficiently fed up
2. **Uncertainty** — Not knowing what comes next; either experimenting or getting lost
3. **Discovery** — Finding what you want to pursue; making "6 years of progress in 6 months"

The protocol below is designed to accelerate passage through all three phases in a single day.

## The One-Day Protocol

### Morning: Psychological Excavation

**Surfacing pain** — four questions to make unconscious dissatisfaction conscious:

- What is the dull, persistent dissatisfaction you've learned to tolerate?
- What do you complain about but never change? (List three.)
- For each complaint: what would an observer of your *behavior* (not words) conclude you actually want?
- What truth about your current life would be unbearable to admit to someone you respect?

**Building the anti-vision** — projecting forward on the current trajectory:

- Describe an average Tuesday in 5 years if nothing changes (body, thoughts, people, 9-to-6, 10pm feeling)
- Same exercise for 10 years: what opportunities closed, who gave up on you?
- End-of-life version: you lived the safe version — what was the cost?
- Who in your life is 5-20 years ahead on your current trajectory? What do you feel looking at them?

**Minimum viable vision:**

- Forget practicality — what does an ideal Tuesday look like in 3 years?
- What would you have to believe about yourself for that life to feel natural? ("I am the type of person who...")
- What is one thing you'd do this week if you were already that person?

### Daytime: Pattern Interrupts

Scheduled self-interrogation reminders throughout the day to break autopilot:

- "What am I avoiding right now by doing what I'm doing?"
- "If someone filmed the last two hours, what would they conclude I want from my life?"
- "Am I moving toward the life I hate or the life I want?"
- "What did I do today out of identity protection rather than genuine desire?"

### Evening: Synthesis

Distill the day's insights into actionable direction:

- What feels most true about why you've been stuck?
- Name the actual enemy — the internal pattern or belief running the show
- One sentence: what you refuse to let your life become (compressed anti-vision)
- One sentence: what you're building toward (vision MVP)

### Goal-Setting as Lens Selection

Goals aren't endpoints — they're lenses that filter perception and guide action:

- **One-year lens:** What would have to be true to know you've broken the old pattern?
- **One-month lens:** What would have to be true for the one-year lens to stay possible?
- **Daily lens:** 2-3 actions the person you're becoming would simply do

## Life as Video Game

The framework organizes all components into a game structure that naturally produces flow states (drawing on Csikszentmihalyi's concept of order in consciousness through goal-directed attention):

| Life component | Game equivalent | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Anti-vision | Stakes | What happens if you lose |
| Vision | Win condition | How you define winning |
| 1-year goal | Mission | Your sole priority |
| 1-month project | Boss fight | How you gain XP |
| Daily levers | Quests | The daily process |
| Constraints | Rules | Limitations that encourage creativity |

These form concentric protective circles that guard attention from distractions. The more you play, the stronger the identity becomes, until the lifestyle feels natural rather than forced — completing the identity-change cycle from the first section.
